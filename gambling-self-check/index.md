# The 9-question gambling self-check (PGSI)

By [Kyrylo Lozovyi](https://trysilex.com/about/) · August 10, 2026

**Short answer:** These nine questions are the **Problem Gambling Severity Index**, the standard screener from the Canadian Problem Gambling Index (Ferris & Wynne, 2001). Score 0–27. A score of 8 or more puts you in the highest-risk band. It runs in your browser — nothing is stored, nothing is sent anywhere, and there is no email box at the end.

The PGSI asks about the **last 12 months**. Answer for that window, not for your worst month ever and not for last week. Being honest with a page that can't remember your answers is the easiest honesty available.

This self-check needs JavaScript to score itself. With JavaScript off, you can still answer the questions below and add them up by hand: Never = 0, Sometimes = 1, Most of the time = 2, Almost always = 3.

This isn't a diagnosis — it's a snapshot of risk based on a standard self-check (PGSI). Scores range from 0 to 27.

Answer all nine questions to see your score.

## How the scoring works

Each of the nine items scores 0 for *never*, 1 for *sometimes*, 2 for *most of the time* and 3 for *almost always*, giving a total from 0 to 27. The bands come from the Canadian Problem Gambling Index:

| Score | Standard band | What it usually means |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Non-problem gambling | No indicators reported in the last 12 months. |
| 1–2 | Low risk | Early signs most casual bettors don't report — worth watching. |
| 3–7 | Moderate risk | A heavier pattern, often with some financial or social consequence already. |
| 8–27 | Problem gambling | The highest band on the screener. Most people here benefit from structured help, not willpower alone. |

Instrument: Ferris, J. & Wynne, H. (2001), *The Canadian Problem Gambling Index: Final Report*, Canadian Centre on Substance Abuse. The PGSI is a screening instrument; clinical diagnosis of gambling disorder uses the DSM-5 criteria and requires a qualified clinician.

## Common questions

### What score means I have a gambling problem?

On the PGSI, 0 is non-problem gambling, 1–2 is low risk, 3–7 is moderate risk, and 8 or above is the band usually described as problem gambling. But the PGSI is a screener, not a diagnosis — only a qualified clinician can diagnose gambling disorder, using the DSM-5 criteria.

### Is this test stored or shared?

No. The scoring happens in JavaScript inside your own browser. There is no form submission, no cookie, no analytics event, and no email capture. Closing the tab erases it.

### Can I take it about someone else?

The PGSI is written in the first person and validated as a self-report, so answering it for a partner or family member gives you a rough impression at best. If you're worried about someone else, the helplines below take calls from family members too.

### What if I score low but still feel out of control?

Trust that feeling over the number. The PGSI weights financial and social consequences heavily, so someone early in a heavy pattern — or someone gambling money they can technically afford — can score low and still be in trouble.
