# How the program was built, and from what

Silex's 90 days were written from published clinical material and research, not from a marketing brief. This page lists what they were built from so you can check the work — and so you can go to the primary sources directly if you'd rather not pay for an app.

## The shape of the program

90 authored days, one lesson at a time, in three phases:

| Phase | Idea | What it draws on |
|---|---|---|
| **See the Machine** | It was engineered. Drop the shame. | Research on how gambling products are designed — variable-ratio reinforcement, losses disguised as wins, near-miss effects, machine-zone immersion |
| **Rewire** | Your brain is resetting. Trust the timeline. | CBT for gambling disorder: trigger mapping, cognitive restructuring of gambling beliefs, urge exposure and urge surfing, behavioural substitution |
| **Fortify** | Build the life that competes. | Relapse-prevention planning, values work, financial repair, rebuilding the activities gambling crowded out |

A lapse doesn't reset anything. It routes you to a **Reset day** built around relapse-prevention material on the abstinence violation effect — the finding that how a person interprets a slip predicts whether it becomes a full return to gambling more reliably than the slip itself does.

## The sources

### Clinical treatment material

- **Nancy M. Petry's cognitive-behavioural work on pathological gambling** — the standard CBT framework for gambling disorder: functional analysis of gambling episodes, trigger identification, coping-skills training and planned alternative activities. Petry's treatment manual and trials are the backbone of Phase 2.
- **The UCLA Gambling Studies Program patient workbook** — a free, publicly available self-help workbook. Several of the day-level exercise formats (trigger inventories, urge logs, decision worksheets) follow its structure.
- **Motivational Interviewing** (William R. Miller & Stephen Rollnick) — why the program asks rather than lectures, and why your own stated reasons for quitting are resurfaced back to you instead of the app supplying motivation.
- **Relapse Prevention** (G. Alan Marlatt & Judith R. Gordon, 1985) — high-risk-situation planning, the abstinence violation effect, and the design rule that a slip must never zero your history.
- **SMART Recovery** and **Gamblers Anonymous** — two different traditions, both pointed to inside the app, because the evidence does not support telling someone there is only one route.

### How gambling products are engineered

- **Natasha Dow Schüll, *Addiction by Design: Machine Gambling in Las Vegas*** (Princeton University Press, 2012) — the ethnography behind Phase 1. The "machine zone", the design of continuous play, and the industry's own language about time-on-device.
- **Anna Lembke, *Dopamine Nation*** (2021) — the pleasure–pain balance framing used to explain why the first weeks feel worse before they feel better.
- **Allen Carr's method** — the argument that the "relief" a bet provides is the withdrawal it created, adapted to gambling rather than smoking.

### Measurement

- **The Problem Gambling Severity Index (PGSI)**, from the Canadian Problem Gambling Index (Ferris & Wynne, 2001) — nine items over a 12-month window, the instrument behind both the app's intake and the [self-check on this site](https://trysilex.com/gambling-self-check/). It is a screening tool: it estimates risk, it does not diagnose anyone.

## What we're careful about

- **The program is not a clinical trial.** Silex applies material from evidence-based treatment; that is not the same as Silex itself having been trialled. Nobody has run a randomised study on this app, and until somebody has, we won't imply otherwise or quote an effectiveness figure.
- **Screening is not diagnosis.** Every risk score in the app and on this site carries the same line: this isn't a diagnosis, it's a snapshot of risk based on a standard self-check.
- **Helplines are re-verified every release**, because the numbers do change — the US national line changed in January 2026.
- **The program is in English only** for now, and its self-exclusion and debt guidance is deepest for the United States and the United Kingdom.

If you spot something in the program that misrepresents a source, write to [support@trysilex.com](mailto:support@trysilex.com) — that is a bug and it gets fixed like one.
