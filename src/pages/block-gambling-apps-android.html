Straight answer first: Silex is on Android now, but you don't need it for anything on this page. It's the setup we'd give a friend on Android, and the free built-in option is better than most of the paid apps.

1. Private DNS: the two-minute one that covers everything

Android has supported encrypted DNS system-wide since Android 9. Point it at a resolver that filters gambling and those domains stop resolving in every app and every browser (sportsbook apps included), on Wi-Fi and on mobile data.

  1. Open Settings → Network & internet (on Samsung: Connections → More connection settings).
  2. Tap Private DNS.
  3. Choose Private DNS provider hostname.
  4. Enter a filtering resolver's hostname, then Save.

Resolvers that filter gambling:

HostnameWhat it filtersCost
family.dns.mullvad.net Gambling, adult content, ads and trackers Free, no account, published no-logging policy
<your-id>.dns.nextdns.io Whatever you switch on (gambling is a one-tap category) Free tier, account needed, gives you logs and control

Test it. Open a browser you don't normally use and try a gambling site you actually used. It should fail to load, not just look odd. If it loads, check that a VPN isn't active. A VPN takes over DNS and silently overrides this setting.

The catch: it's a Settings toggle, so you can undo it in about a minute. That's still meaningful (most relapses are impulsive and a minute of friction stops a lot of them), but it is not a wall. Which is what the next steps are for.

2. Put a PIN on it that isn't yours

This is the step that turns a speed bump into something sturdier. Uninstall every betting app first. Then have a partner, a friend or someone in your family choose the PIN and keep it.

  • Android 17 and later: Parental controls. Settings → Parental controls → Set up Parental controls PIN. App store filters → Google Play Store limits new installs by age rating, and Google requires real-money gambling apps to carry an adults-only rating. App limits blocks an app that's still on the phone. While the controls are on, adding users and resetting the phone ask for the PIN too. The recovery account can reset the PIN, so it should be theirs, not yours (Google's steps).
  • Older Android: an App limits PIN. Settings → Digital Wellbeing → App limits → App limits PIN. With a PIN someone else keeps, a limit of zero blocks the app. Without a PIN, app timers and Focus mode are trivially reversible.
  • Google Play's own content restrictions. Play Store → your profile icon → Settings → Family → Content restrictions. An older Play PIN can be reset by clearing the Play Store's data, so treat it as friction.

Not Family Link. It gets recommended a lot, but Google describes it for children and teens, and only under-18s need a parent's approval to stop it. It isn't built to hold between two adults.

Menus differ between phone makers, and none of these is documented to lock Private DNS itself.

4. The two layers that aren't on your phone at all

Same on every platform, and stronger than anything above:

  1. Self-exclusion: free, and you can't reverse it during a craving. GAMSTOP in the UK; state programme plus each operator in the US.
  2. Bank gambling block: free, typically 48–72 hours to lift.

If you only have ten minutes today, do these two and come back to the phone settings afterwards.

What doesn't work on Android

  • Uninstalling the apps alone. The mobile site works, and reinstalling takes seconds.
  • Router or Wi-Fi filtering by itself. Ends at your front door; mobile data walks straight past it.
  • A plain VPN. Not a filter, and it can disable your Private DNS setting.
  • Blocking one browser. Android will happily install another in twenty seconds.

Then the part no blocker does

Every layer above buys you time during an urge, and urges pass in roughly 20 to 30 minutes whether or not you bet. What you do with those minutes is what decides whether this holds: riding out an urge, and the wider plan. Neither of those needs an iPhone or a subscription.
