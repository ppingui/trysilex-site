Every other layer is something you install and can uninstall. Self-exclusion is different: you hand the decision to someone else, and they will not give it back on demand. That is why it is the first thing worth doing, and the thing most people put off longest.

UK: one registration, every licensed site

GAMSTOP is the national online self-exclusion scheme. It is free, takes a few minutes, and every operator licensed by the Gambling Commission to offer online gambling in Britain is required to participate. Once you're registered, they must close your accounts and refuse new ones.

  • Choose your term: 6 months, 1 year or 5 years. Take 5 years. The reason to choose a short term is almost always the part of you that intends to come back.
  • It doesn't expire quietly. Reaching the end date does not restore access by itself — you have to contact GAMSTOP and pass a cooling-off period first. Verify the current process on their site; it has been revised before.
  • Register accurately. Matching is done on your details, so an old address or a variant spelling can leave an account uncovered. Include every email address you have gambled with.
  • Betting shops are separate. GAMSTOP is online only, and says so — the in-person scheme is a different sign-up.

The gap, stated honestly

GAMSTOP binds UK-licensed operators. It has no power over sites licensed elsewhere, and an entire SEO industry exists to put "casinos not on GAMSTOP" in front of people at the exact moment they are looking for a way around their own decision. Those operators sit outside UK consumer protection, dispute resolution and deposit safeguards.

Two things close that gap: a DNS-level blocker, which doesn't care whose licence a site holds, and a bank gambling block, which doesn't care what site you reached. Self-exclusion plus those two is the combination that actually holds.

US: state scheme plus every operator

There is no national equivalent of GAMSTOP. Coverage is built in two layers:

  1. Your state programme. Most states with legal gambling run a voluntary self-exclusion list, administered by the gaming commission or lottery, often covering both casinos and licensed online operators in that state. Terms are typically one year, five years or lifetime. The National Council on Problem Gambling keeps a directory of state self-exclusion programmes.
  2. Each operator, individually. Every licensed sportsbook and casino app has a responsible-gaming section with self-exclusion or self-restriction. Do it for all of them, including the ones you have "stopped using" — a dormant funded account is the easiest relapse there is.

Where you can, choose the longest term offered, and do it in one sitting rather than one operator a week. Momentum matters more than thoroughness here; you can add the ones you forgot tomorrow.

Elsewhere

Several countries run national schemes — among them Sweden (Spelpaus), the Netherlands (Cruks), Denmark (ROFUS), Belgium (EPIS) and Australia (BetStop). If you're outside those, ask every operator you use directly for permanent self-exclusion; licensed operators almost everywhere are obliged to offer it. Gambling Therapy provides free online support internationally and can point you at what exists locally.

What to expect after you do it

  • Marketing should stop. Excluded customers must be removed from promotional contact. If an operator keeps emailing or texting you, that's a compliance failure worth reporting to the regulator — and worth doing, because those messages are engineered to arrive when you're vulnerable.
  • Withdraw your balances first, or at least check what happens to them under each operator's terms.
  • Expect a bad week. Removing the option tends to make the wanting louder before it makes it quieter. That's the withdrawal pattern, not a sign you chose wrong — here's the shape it usually takes.
  • Tell one person. Self-exclusion is easier to keep when it isn't a secret, and secrecy is one of the heaviest parts of a gambling problem to carry alone.

The uncomfortable question

If reading this you found yourself thinking "I'll do the six months, not the five years" — notice that. It is the most reliable signal in the whole process, and it is worth being honest with yourself about which part of you is doing the negotiating.
