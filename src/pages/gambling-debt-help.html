Gambling debt has one feature that makes it different from ordinary debt: the thing that caused it looks, from the inside, like the fastest way to fix it. That belief is what turns a manageable number into an unmanageable one, and it is the first thing to disarm.

1. Stop the bleeding before you do the arithmetic

There's no point building a repayment plan around a balance that's still moving. Both of these are free and take under twenty minutes:

  • Self-exclude from every operator — GAMSTOP covers all UK-licensed sites in one registration; in the US, your state programme plus each sportsbook.
  • Bank gambling block — declines payments to gambling merchants and takes 48–72 hours to lift.

Then block it on your phone. In that order: strongest first.

2. Get all of it onto one page

Most people carrying gambling debt have never seen the full number, because it's spread across cards, overdrafts, an app loan, a payday lender and three people they can't face. The fragmentation is doing you harm — it makes the total feel survivable in a way that keeps you from acting.

Write down, for each one: who it's owed to, how much, the interest rate, the minimum payment. Include money owed to family and friends, even though it has no paperwork. Include anything borrowed against your salary.

Then separate them. Debts where the consequence is losing your home, your utilities, your transport to work or your liberty come first — in UK debt advice these are called priority debts, and they get dealt with ahead of credit cards regardless of who is shouting loudest. A debt adviser will do this triage with you in one call, and it is the part people most often get wrong alone.

3. Call a free debt charity

This is the highest-value phone call available to you, and the thing people delay for months out of shame.

  • UK: StepChange and National Debtline. Free, charity-run, no judgement, and they'll talk to creditors for you.
  • US: NFCC member agencies — non-profit credit counselling, free initial counselling.
  • Either: the national gambling helplines will point you at local debt help as part of the same call. US 1-800-MY-RESET, UK 0808 8020 133.

What they can arrange that you generally can't alone: frozen interest, paused collection, affordable repayment plans, and — in England and Wales — the Breathing Space scheme, which stops most interest, fees and enforcement action for 60 days while you get advice. Ask whether you qualify.

4. What to avoid

  • Consolidation while you're still gambling. It converts several maxed balances into one loan plus several empty credit lines. This is how a £10,000 problem becomes a £25,000 one, and it happens to people who were genuinely trying to fix it.
  • Anyone charging a fee to "sort" gambling debt. Free charities do the same work better. Paid debt-relief outfits target this exact search.
  • Borrowing from family to clear it in one go. If you're still gambling, it reloads rather than resolves — and it converts a financial problem into a relationship one. (It's also why families are advised not to clear gambling debts. If someone has said no to you, that's why — it isn't abandonment.)
  • Any plan that requires a win. Including "one last session to get back to level". This is the belief that built the debt, wearing a suit.
  • Crypto, high-risk trading, or a "system". Same loop, different interface.

5. Expect the debt itself to be a trigger

This is the part standard debt advice misses. Statements, calls and the size of the number produce exactly the stress state that used to be answered with a bet — which is why the blocks in step 1 need to be up before you start opening envelopes.

Two things help: a repayment plan you didn't design alone, so the number stops being an emergency and becomes a schedule; and knowing that the urge to fix it in one night peaks and passes like any other. Usually in 20 to 30 minutes.

6. Watch the number move the other way

Once the losses stop, the money you're not gambling starts doing something. Seeing that as a rising figure rather than an absence is a surprisingly durable motivator — it's why Silex counts the money you kept instead of the days you white-knuckled.
