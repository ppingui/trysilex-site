# Gambling debt: where to start

By [Kyrylo Lozovyi](https://trysilex.com/about/) · August 10, 2026

**Short answer:** In order: **stop new losses** (self-exclusion and a bank block, both free and both today), **write down every debt in one place** including the money owed to people, then **call a free debt charity** — StepChange or National Debtline in the UK, an NFCC member agency in the US. They are free, they don't judge, and they get better outcomes than anything you can arrange alone at 2am. Do not take a consolidation loan while you are still gambling.

Gambling debt has one feature that makes it different from ordinary debt: the thing that caused it looks, from the inside, like the fastest way to fix it. That belief is what turns a manageable number into an unmanageable one, and it is the first thing to disarm.

## 1. Stop the bleeding before you do the arithmetic

There's no point building a repayment plan around a balance that's still moving. Both of these are free and take under twenty minutes:

- [**Self-exclude**](https://trysilex.com/self-exclusion-guide/) from every operator — GAMSTOP covers all UK-licensed sites in one registration; in the US, your state programme plus each sportsbook.
- [**Bank gambling block**](https://trysilex.com/block-gambling-transactions-bank/) — declines payments to gambling merchants and takes 48–72 hours to lift.

Then [block it on your phone](https://trysilex.com/block-gambling-sites-iphone/). In that order: strongest first.

## 2. Get all of it onto one page

Most people carrying gambling debt have never seen the full number, because it's spread across cards, overdrafts, an app loan, a payday lender and three people they can't face. The fragmentation is doing you harm — it makes the total feel survivable in a way that keeps you from acting.

Write down, for each one: who it's owed to, how much, the interest rate, the minimum payment. Include money owed to family and friends, even though it has no paperwork. Include anything borrowed against your salary.

Then separate them. Debts where the consequence is losing your home, your utilities, your transport to work or your liberty come first — in UK debt advice these are called **priority debts**, and they get dealt with ahead of credit cards regardless of who is shouting loudest. A debt adviser will do this triage with you in one call, and it is the part people most often get wrong alone.

## 3. Call a free debt charity

This is the highest-value phone call available to you, and the thing people delay for months out of shame.

- **UK:** [StepChange](https://www.stepchange.org) and [National Debtline](https://nationaldebtline.org). Free, charity-run, no judgement, and they'll talk to creditors for you.
- **US:** [NFCC](https://www.nfcc.org) member agencies — non-profit credit counselling, free initial counselling.
- **Either:** the national gambling helplines will point you at local debt help as part of the same call. US [1-800-MY-RESET](tel:18006973738), UK [0808 8020 133](tel:08088020133).

What they can arrange that you generally can't alone: frozen interest, paused collection, affordable repayment plans, and — in England and Wales — the **Breathing Space** scheme, which stops most interest, fees and enforcement action for 60 days while you get advice. Ask whether you qualify.

## 4. What to avoid

- **Consolidation while you're still gambling.** It converts several maxed balances into one loan plus several empty credit lines. This is how a £10,000 problem becomes a £25,000 one, and it happens to people who were genuinely trying to fix it.
- **Anyone charging a fee to "sort" gambling debt.** Free charities do the same work better. Paid debt-relief outfits target this exact search.
- **Borrowing from family to clear it in one go.** If you're still gambling, it reloads rather than resolves — and it converts a financial problem into a relationship one. (It's also why [families are advised not to clear gambling debts](https://trysilex.com/help-someone-with-gambling-addiction/). If someone has said no to you, that's why — it isn't abandonment.)
- **Any plan that requires a win.** Including "one last session to get back to level". This is the belief that built the debt, wearing a suit.
- **Crypto, high-risk trading, or a "system".** Same loop, different interface.

## 5. Expect the debt itself to be a trigger

This is the part standard debt advice misses. Statements, calls and the size of the number produce exactly the stress state that used to be answered with a bet — which is why the blocks in step 1 need to be up *before* you start opening envelopes.

Two things help: a repayment plan you didn't design alone, so the number stops being an emergency and becomes a schedule; and knowing that the urge to fix it in one night peaks and passes like any other. [Usually in 20 to 30 minutes.](https://trysilex.com/how-long-do-gambling-urges-last/)

## 6. Watch the number move the other way

Once the losses stop, the money you're not gambling starts doing something. Seeing that as a rising figure rather than an absence is a surprisingly durable motivator — it's why Silex counts the money you kept instead of the days you white-knuckled. ## If the debt has you thinking about not being here

Gambling debt and suicidal thoughts are strongly linked, and financial shame is a particularly isolating kind. Please use the numbers below — in the US, 988; in the UK, Samaritans on 116 123. Debt is solvable, including debt that currently looks unsolvable, and free advisers deal with worse numbers than yours every day of the week.

## Common questions

### Can I get help with gambling debt for free?

Yes, and free help is better than paid help here. StepChange and National Debtline in the UK, and NFCC member agencies in the US, are non-profit, charge nothing, and will negotiate with creditors on your behalf. Anyone charging you a fee upfront to fix gambling debt is selling you something you can get for nothing.

### Should I take a loan to pay off gambling debt?

Not while you are still gambling — a consolidation loan clears balances that then become fresh credit lines, which is the single most common way gambling debt doubles. Once you have genuinely stopped and the blocks are in place, a debt adviser can tell you whether consolidation makes sense for your particular situation. That is their job, and they have no product to sell you.

### Will creditors know it was gambling?

You don't have to itemise the cause, but telling a debt adviser is worth it — they'll factor it in, and many lenders have vulnerability policies that can mean frozen interest or paused collection. In England and Wales, a debt adviser can also tell you whether you qualify for the Breathing Space scheme, which pauses most interest and enforcement for 60 days.

### Should I tell my family about the debt?

Almost always, and sooner is easier than later — the number only grows while it's secret, and secrecy is the thing that makes gambling debt escalate. It's worth speaking to a helpline first to plan how, especially if housing or children are involved.

### Does bankruptcy clear gambling debt?

In many places gambling debts are treated like other unsecured debts in insolvency, but the rules, the consequences and the alternatives differ a lot by country and by situation, and there are usually less drastic options. This is exactly the decision to take to a free debt adviser rather than to a forum.
