# Self-exclusion: the strongest block there is

By [Kyrylo Lozovyi](https://trysilex.com/about/) · August 10, 2026

**Short answer:** Self-exclusion means telling operators to close your accounts and refuse you service for a fixed term. In the UK, one free **GAMSTOP** registration covers every UK-licensed online operator for **6 months, 1 year or 5 years**, and it does not lift automatically at the end. In the US, register with your **state programme** and with each sportsbook individually. It is free, it takes about twenty minutes, and unlike any app you cannot reverse it during a craving.

Every other layer is something you install and can uninstall. Self-exclusion is different: you hand the decision to someone else, and they will not give it back on demand. That is why it is the first thing worth doing, and the thing most people put off longest.

## UK: one registration, every licensed site

**[GAMSTOP](https://www.gamstop.co.uk/)** is the national online self-exclusion scheme. It is free, takes a few minutes, and every operator licensed by the Gambling Commission to offer online gambling in Britain is required to participate. Once you're registered, they must close your accounts and refuse new ones.

- **Choose your term:** 6 months, 1 year or 5 years. Take 5 years. The reason to choose a short term is almost always the part of you that intends to come back.
- **It doesn't expire quietly.** Reaching the end date does not restore access by itself — you have to contact GAMSTOP and pass a cooling-off period first. Verify the current process on their site; it has been revised before.
- **Register accurately.** Matching is done on your details, so an old address or a variant spelling can leave an account uncovered. Include every email address you have gambled with.
- **Betting shops are separate.** GAMSTOP is online only, and says so — the in-person scheme is a different sign-up.

### The gap, stated honestly

GAMSTOP binds UK-licensed operators. It has no power over sites licensed elsewhere, and an entire SEO industry exists to put "casinos not on GAMSTOP" in front of people at the exact moment they are looking for a way around their own decision. Those operators sit outside UK consumer protection, dispute resolution and deposit safeguards.

Two things close that gap: a [DNS-level blocker](https://trysilex.com/block-gambling-sites-iphone/), which doesn't care whose licence a site holds, and a [bank gambling block](https://trysilex.com/block-gambling-transactions-bank/), which doesn't care what site you reached. Self-exclusion plus those two is the combination that actually holds.

## US: state scheme plus every operator

There is no national equivalent of GAMSTOP. Coverage is built in two layers:

1. **Your state programme.** Most states with legal gambling run a voluntary self-exclusion list, administered by the gaming commission or lottery, often covering both casinos and licensed online operators in that state. Terms are typically one year, five years or lifetime. The National Council on Problem Gambling keeps a [directory of state self-exclusion programmes](https://www.ncpgambling.org/help-treatment/self-exclusion/).
2. **Each operator, individually.** Every licensed sportsbook and casino app has a responsible-gaming section with self-exclusion or self-restriction. Do it for all of them, including the ones you have "stopped using" — a dormant funded account is the easiest relapse there is. ([Quitting sports betting specifically](https://trysilex.com/how-to-stop-sports-betting/) has its own problem: the cue is the sport, and you can't self-exclude from that.)

Where you can, choose the longest term offered, and do it in one sitting rather than one operator a week. Momentum matters more than thoroughness here; you can add the ones you forgot tomorrow.

## Elsewhere

Several countries run national schemes — among them Sweden (Spelpaus), the Netherlands (Cruks), Denmark (ROFUS), Belgium (EPIS) and Australia (BetStop). If you're outside those, ask every operator you use directly for permanent self-exclusion; licensed operators almost everywhere are obliged to offer it. [Gambling Therapy](https://www.gamblingtherapy.org) provides free online support internationally and can point you at what exists locally.

## What to expect after you do it

- **Marketing should stop.** Excluded customers must be removed from promotional contact. If an operator keeps emailing or texting you, that's a compliance failure worth reporting to the regulator — and worth doing, because those messages are engineered to arrive when you're vulnerable.
- **Withdraw your balances first,** or at least check what happens to them under each operator's terms.
- **Expect a bad week.** Removing the option tends to make the wanting louder before it makes it quieter. That's the withdrawal pattern, not a sign you chose wrong — [here's the shape it usually takes](https://trysilex.com/how-long-do-gambling-urges-last/).
- **Tell one person.** Self-exclusion is easier to keep when it isn't a secret, and secrecy is one of the heaviest parts of a gambling problem to carry alone. (If you're the person being told: [what actually helps](https://trysilex.com/help-someone-with-gambling-addiction/).)

## The uncomfortable question

If reading this you found yourself thinking "I'll do the six months, not the five years" — notice that. It is the most reliable signal in the whole process, and it is worth being honest with yourself about which part of you is doing the negotiating.

## Common questions

### How long does GAMSTOP last?

You choose 6 months, 1 year or 5 years. At the end of the term it does not switch off by itself — you have to contact GAMSTOP and pass a cooling-off period before access is restored. Check GAMSTOP's own current terms, because the reactivation process has changed before.

### Does GAMSTOP cover betting shops?

No. GAMSTOP is the online scheme, covering websites and apps of UK-licensed operators. Betting shops are a separate self-exclusion process, and GAMSTOP itself points people towards it.

### Can I gamble on sites not covered by GAMSTOP?

Technically yes, and that is the scheme's real weakness — it has no authority over operators outside the UK licence. There is an entire industry of 'non-GAMSTOP casino' sites built to catch people mid-relapse, and unlicensed operators offer no UK consumer protection at all. This is exactly the gap a DNS-level blocker covers.

### How do I self-exclude from a US sportsbook?

Each operator has a responsible-gaming section with a self-exclusion or self-restriction option, usually offering set terms. Do it per operator, and separately register with your state programme where one exists — the National Council on Problem Gambling maintains a directory of state schemes.

### Will self-exclusion hurt my credit score?

No. Self-exclusion is an agreement with gambling operators or a scheme they subscribe to. It is not a credit event and does not appear on a credit file.
