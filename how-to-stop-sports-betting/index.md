# How to stop sports betting

By [Kyrylo Lozovyi](https://trysilex.com/about/) · August 10, 2026

**Short answer:** Sports betting has a problem other gambling doesn't: the trigger is a thing you love and don't want to give up. So blocking the apps isn't enough — you also have to break the link between *watching* and *betting*. Close every account properly (self-exclusion, not deletion), block at the DNS and bank level, kill the odds notifications, and expect the first month of fixtures to be the hardest part.

Quitting a casino app means avoiding a place. Quitting sports betting means staying in a place you like — the sport, the group chat, the Saturday — while removing one thing from it. That's a harder problem, and most generic quit-gambling advice ignores it entirely.

## Why this one's different

- **The cue is everywhere and you don't want it gone.** Odds are read out during coverage, printed on the screen, embedded in the highlights app and pushed to your phone. Nobody expects you to stop caring about football.
- **It feels like skill.** That's the trap unique to sports. When a slots player loses, they know it was chance. When a bettor loses, they conclude they misread the injury news — so the answer feels like *more research*, which means more bets.
- **It's social.** The group chat with everyone's slips in it is a genuine friendship, and quitting can feel like leaving it.
- **Live betting removed the gaps.** In-play markets turned a single pre-match bet into a decision every few minutes for ninety minutes.

## Step 1: close the accounts properly

Deleting apps does almost nothing — the mobile site works, reinstalling takes seconds, and your balance and card are still sitting there. **Self-exclude instead.** It closes the account, stops them serving you, and can't be undone in a bad moment.

- **UK:** one free **GAMSTOP** registration covers every UK-licensed online operator, for 6 months, 1 year or 5 years. Betting shops are a separate scheme — do that too if you use them.
- **US:** each sportsbook has self-exclusion in its responsible-gaming settings, and most states run their own programme. Do every operator you hold an account with, dormant ones included.

## Step 2: block the routes back

1. **Device-wide DNS blocking.** Covers every app and browser, on Wi-Fi and cellular — not just Safari. [Setup on iPhone →](https://trysilex.com/block-gambling-sites-iphone/) [on Android →](https://trysilex.com/block-gambling-apps-android/)
2. **A bank gambling block.** Most UK banks offer one in-app with a 48–72 hour cooling-off; in the US, ask your issuer about merchant code 7995. [How →](https://trysilex.com/block-gambling-transactions-bank/)
3. **Kill the notifications.** This is the sports-specific one everyone skips. Turn off push from every sportsbook *and* from score apps that carry odds. Unsubscribe from betting emails. Mute the odds segments where you can.

## Step 3: break the watching–betting link

This is the part that decides whether it holds past the first month.

Betting and watching became one activity. Until they're separate again, every kickoff is a craving cue. Practical version:

- **Take a few weeks off live games.** Not forever — long enough that "match on" stops meaning "slip on". Highlights are much weaker cues than live.
- **Change what you do with your hands.** Watch with someone, at a friend's, at a pub, anywhere your phone isn't the second screen. The living-room-alone-with-phone setup is the highest-risk configuration there is.
- **Give the stake a job.** Some people move their usual stake into a savings pot at kickoff. It sounds gimmicky and it works, because the ritual survives while the outcome stops being financial.
- **Plan the group chat.** You don't owe anyone an explanation, but decide what you'll say before it comes up. "I've stopped betting, still here for the football" ends the conversation faster than dodging it for a month.

## Step 4: the first month of fixtures

Expect predictable spikes rather than a smooth decline — the start of a season, a derby, a final, a big race meeting, and the moment your accumulator would have come in. Urges peak and pass in roughly 20 to 30 minutes whether or not you bet. [The urge-surfing method is here →](https://trysilex.com/how-long-do-gambling-urges-last/)

One specific trap for bettors: **the near-miss you'd have won.** You will, early on, watch a result land that would have paid. That's not evidence you should have bet — it's the same variable reward that kept you in, arriving without costing you anything this time. Notice it, name it, let it pass.

## What to do about the money already lost

Sports bettors tend to be chasing more than casino players, because "I know this sport" makes recovery feel achievable. It isn't, and chasing is where most of the real damage happens. ## Common questions

### Why is sports betting so hard to quit?

Because the cue never goes away. Someone quitting slots can avoid casinos; you can't avoid sport without giving up something you actually enjoy, and every match, highlight reel and group chat carries a betting prompt with it. On top of that, sports betting feels like skill rather than chance, so losses read as bad research instead of an expected outcome — which makes chasing feel rational.

### Can I keep watching sport if I quit betting?

Most people can, eventually, but not usually in month one. The honest sequence is: stop betting first, take a break from live-watching for a few weeks while the association weakens, then come back to the sport deliberately. If you go straight back to watching with the app still on your phone, you're testing willpower during the exact ninety seconds it's weakest.

### Should I delete the app or self-exclude?

Self-exclude. Deleting the app takes fifteen seconds to undo and leaves your account funded and waiting. Self-exclusion closes the account and stops them serving you, and you cannot reverse it in a craving. Do it with every sportsbook you hold an account with, including the dormant ones.

### What about free bets and promos they send when I stop?

Expect them, and expect them to be well timed — operators run reactivation campaigns at people who have gone quiet. Self-exclusion should remove you from marketing; if the messages keep coming, that is a compliance failure worth reporting to the regulator. Unsubscribe, block the sender, and turn off every push notification from sports apps too.

### Is fantasy sports or prediction-market trading a safe substitute?

For most people in recovery, no. Daily fantasy with entry fees, and prediction markets with sports contracts, deliver the same loop — stake, uncertainty, outcome tied to a match — with a different label on the button. If the appeal is having something riding on the game, that's the pattern to break, not to re-house.
