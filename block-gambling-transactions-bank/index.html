How to make your bank decline gambling payments

By Kyrylo Lozovyi · August 10, 2026

Short answer: Most major UK banks let you switch on a gambling block in the app, and it takes a 48 to 72-hour cooling-off period to switch back off — which is exactly why it works. In the US there is no consumer toggle at most banks, but gambling deposits on credit cards are already widely declined under merchant category code 7995, and you can ask your issuer to block that code.

Blocking websites stops you reaching the bet. Blocking payments stops you funding it, and it is the harder of the two to undo — which makes it the more valuable one. A gambling block at your bank refuses transactions to gambling merchants outright, and reversing it takes days rather than seconds.

Why the delay is the feature

Every layer worth having is really a bet on time. Gambling urges are impulsive and short-lived; a barrier that takes one minute to remove often fails, and a barrier that takes two days almost never does. When a bank makes you wait 48 hours to re-enable gambling payments, it has converted a decision made in the worst possible state of mind into one made two days later, calm.

The research on card blockers makes the same point about design details — the University of Bristol's Personal Finance Research Centre published a blueprint for bank card gambling blockers arguing that friction to unblock matters as much as the block itself.

UK: turn it on in the app

Most UK current accounts now include a gambling block, usually under card controls or spending limits in the mobile app. Reported behaviour, which you should confirm with your own bank because these terms change:

If your bank isn't listed, phone them and ask directly for a gambling transaction block — coverage across UK banks and building societies is now wide, and the frontline staff at most of them handle this request routinely and without any awkwardness. GamCare maintains a plain-language explainer of how bank gambling blocks work.

Pick the longest period you can stand. Monzo's up-to-12-months option is the strongest single money-side commitment available from a UK bank, and it is free.

US: what actually exists

There is no widely available "block gambling" switch in US banking apps. What exists instead:

  • Credit cards mostly already decline it. Online gambling merchants are assigned merchant category code 7995, and major US issuers treat gambling deposits as cash advances or cash equivalents — several blanket-decline the code. If you have been funding gambling by credit card, this may already be closing.
  • Ask your issuer to block MCC 7995 permanently. Say exactly that: "please place a permanent block on merchant category code 7995 on my account." Some banks will do it, some will tell you it is already in place, and a few will say they can't — in which case ask for the request to be noted.
  • Remove the cards from the operator's side. Delete saved cards and bank links from every sportsbook and casino account you have. Do it in the same sitting as self-exclusion, because self-exclusion closes the account that holds them.
  • Watch the e-wallet gap. PayPal, Venmo, Cash App, prepaid cards and crypto on-ramps are the usual detour when a card stops working. Closing or unlinking those matters as much as the card block.

What a bank block does not cover

  • Miscategorised merchants. The block reads the merchant's category code. Operators occasionally sit under a code that doesn't trip it.
  • Intermediaries. A payment to an e-wallet is a payment to an e-wallet as far as your bank is concerned, whatever happens next.
  • Cash, and other people's money. No bank control reaches an ATM withdrawal, a friend's card, or borrowing.
  • Another account. Blocking one bank while holding an unblocked account somewhere else does very little. Block them all, including the one you barely use.

Do these three in one sitting

  1. The bank block — on every account and card you hold, not just the main one.
  2. Self-exclusion — which closes the accounts your cards are saved in.
  3. Phone-level blocking — so the sites stop loading in the first place.

Twenty minutes, no cost, and between them they remove almost every route that a craving takes when it is looking for one. Then comes the part that actually decides the outcome — what you do with the urge once the door is shut. That's here.

If gambling has left debt behind

Blocking payments doesn't address money already owed, and debt pressure is one of the most reliable triggers for chasing losses. Free, non-judgemental advice: StepChange and National Debtline in the UK, NFCC member agencies in the US. None of them charge, and talking to one is not a commitment to anything.

Common questions

Which UK banks have a gambling block?

Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, NatWest, Santander, Monzo and Starling all offer one, along with several others. Reported cooling-off periods before the block can be lifted range from 48 hours (Starling, Lloyds, NatWest) to 72 hours (Barclays), and Monzo lets you set anything from 2 days to 12 months. Confirm the current terms with your own bank — they change.

Does a gambling block stop every gambling payment?

No. It works on the merchant category the operator is registered under, so a payment routed through an e-wallet, a crypto exchange or a merchant miscategorised as something else can slip past. It also does not stop cash. Treat it as a strong layer, not a seal.

Can I block gambling on a US bank card?

Rarely as a self-service switch. But gambling deposits on US credit cards are commonly declined already — issuers treat them as cash advances or cash equivalents and many blanket-decline merchant code 7995. Call your issuer and ask for a permanent block on that code; some will set it, and some will simply confirm it is already in place.

Will the block affect anything else?

Occasionally. Merchant categorisation is imperfect, so a small number of legitimate payments — a lottery-adjacent retailer, an arcade, some prize-draw sites — can be caught. That is a minor inconvenience against the thing it prevents.

Is a bank block better than a blocker app?

It is stronger, because you cannot lift it in the ninety seconds an urge lasts. It is also narrower, because it only touches money movement, not access. Use both.
